Copyright, copycats & takedown

Last updated: October 27, 2026

Ebice is a free tool, not free content to re-sell. This page says exactly what belongs to whom, what you may do with it, and the quickest way to resolve a claim — with or without a lawyer. This page is a policy, not legal advice.

1. What is protected

Unless a file says otherwise, everything in this repository and on ebice.site is all rights reserved by the owner of Ebice. That includes:

  • the interface: layout, styling, colour system, icons drawn for the site, and the wording of every page;
  • the obfuscation engine — the JavaScript that renames symbols, encodes strings and injects control flow;
  • the Ebice name, the “E” mark, the logo and the social preview image (og-image.png, favicon.png);
  • the legal pages themselves, including this one.

The site is published from a public repository so users can audit what runs on their machine. Audit is not a licence: reading the code is allowed, republishing it as your own product is not. See the Terms of Service, section 5.

2. What you may do

  • Link to https://ebice.site/ from anywhere, including commercial pages, with no permission needed.
  • Quote a short extract (a few sentences or one screenshot) with a visible credit and a link to the canonical page.
  • Use and share the output of the obfuscator. The generated Lua is yours; the header comment inside it is a technical marker, not a claim over your code.
  • Fork privately for your own experiments. Public mirrors, “ Ebice 2.0” clones and paid wrappers are not licensed.

Anything else — hosting the site under another name or domain, removing the copyright notice, keeping our AdSense id while charging for access, packaging the engine as an app or a Discord bot, or scraping the pages into a dataset or a model — needs written permission. Ask, the answer is often yes with attribution.

3. What stays yours

You keep all rights to the Lua you paste in and to the code that comes back out. Ebice stores nothing, uploads nothing, and claims nothing. There is no account, no database and no server-side copy of your script, so there is nothing here for anyone to take from you.

Two markers exist in the output and both are switches you can turn off in “Output & privacy”:

  • Signed header — a comment naming the tool, the active layers and a timestamp. It advertises the tool that produced the file; it does not transfer anything.
  • Ownership stamp — a short one-way fingerprint of your source text, so if a script leaks you can show that a given file was produced from a given source. It is not a signature, not encryption, and it contains no name, no e-mail, no machine and no file name.

If someone takes your obfuscated script, the stamp does not prove who wrote it — it proves which source produced which output. Keep your own repository history; that is the part that wins arguments.

4. Found a copycat?

Ebice is a static page, so anything “installed” on the client is a deterrent, not a wall. What the site already does about copies, and what it cannot do:

  • an unknown domain gets a notice and is sent back to ebice.site;
  • any AdSense publisher id that is not the owner’s is deleted before it can bill;
  • a copy that strips the guard file logs a warning and loses its “genuine domain” marker;
  • framing the page inside someone else’s site is refused;
  • what no client-side trick can stop: a determined copy-paster with an editor. That is what the two routes below are for.

Report a copycat to contact [at] ebice.site with the URL, a screenshot and one line about how it is being used. Every report is read. Where the host offers a copyright or impersonation process, that is the fastest lever, and a public record of the request is often enough on its own.

If you are the copycat and would rather not deal with any of this

Say so in the same address. A working, credited mirror is usually settled in one e-mail: keep the notice, link back, do not charge for access, and the report never has to be filed.

5. Sending a takedown notice

For material on this site that infringes your copyright, send a written notice containing all six items below (the requirements of 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3)). A notice without them usually cannot be acted on — that is the law, not a policy choice:

  1. identification of the copyrighted work you own (title, and where the original is published);
  2. the exact URL(s) on ebice.site and enough detail to find the material;
  3. your name, postal address, telephone number and e-mail;
  4. a statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorised by you, your agent or the law;
  5. a statement, under penalty of perjury, that the information above is accurate and that you are the owner or authorised to act for the owner;
  6. your physical or electronic signature.

Send it by e-mail to contact [at] ebice.site with the subject line DMCA — <page>. This is a static site: removals are usually done the same day, and a public copy is replaced on the next deploy. Misrepresentation claims are on the sender, so do not file a notice to settle a dispute over who wrote a Lua script.

Hosting does not give the copyright holder much to work with either: GitHub, which serves this repository, has its own DMCA process at docs.github.com and it is faster to escalate there than to fight the page itself.

6. Counter-notice

If you believe something was removed by mistake or that your use is lawful, reply with: your contact details, the URL of the removed material, a statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good-faith belief it was removed by error or misidentification, and your consent to the jurisdiction of the district court for where you live (or, outside the United States, any district where the host may be found). The material is restored after 10–14 business days unless the claimant tells us they have filed an action.

7. Bots and AI harvesters

robots.txt disallows the known training and data-harvesting crawlers, and the pages carry noai / noimageai signals. Those are instructions, not locks: a crawler that ignores them is still scraping, and no licence to train on, mirror or re-host Ebice is granted by anything on this site. Bulk fetching that degrades the service is also a breach of section 3 of the Terms.
